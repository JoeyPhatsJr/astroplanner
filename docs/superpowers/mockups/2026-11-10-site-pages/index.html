How it works

Every number shows its work.

The sky, for your spot

Sun, Moon and target positions are computed with Astropy for your saved latitude, longitude and elevation, every five minutes through the night. Times are worked out in UTC and shown in your own timezone. A night runs from local noon to noon, so an evening that crosses midnight stays one night.

The results are checked automatically against independent references: US Naval Observatory sunrise, sunset, twilight and moonrise across both 2026 clock changes, a southern-hemisphere site, a midnight-sun case, USNO Moon phases and a worked example from Meeus.

Tonight’s Score

A 0 to 100 planning estimate for the best two hours of darkness. Each part says whether it comes from a forecast or a calculation.

Cloud cover45 × (1 − cloud %)
Forecast45
Rain risk25 × (1 − precipitation probability)
Forecast25
Moonlight20 × (1 − illuminated fraction) while it is up
Calculated20
Darkness available10 × min(dark minutes / 360, 1)
Calculated10

No forecast, no total. If the weather can’t be fetched, you see the Moon and darkness parts on their own, out of 30, never a made-up 100. Missing forecast hours are skipped, not treated as clear.

Ranking targets

Only targets with at least 30 minutes above your minimum altitude in darkness are ranked. Each is scored over its longest window:

Altitudehigher means less air to shoot through
Calculated30
Imaging timefull points at 4 hours
Calculated25
Weather in the windowcloud and rain for those hours
Forecast25
Moon interferenceits brightness and distance; gentler on emission nebulae
Calculated20

Without a forecast the weather part is left out and the score is shown out of 75, not stretched to look like 100.

What it won’t claim

  • Scores are estimates, not sky quality. They don’t measure transparency or seeing, and say so on screen.
  • Framing is approximate. The Seestar S30’s published 2.46° field doesn’t say which axis it is, so “larger than the field” notes are advice and never change a score.
  • The Seestar’s range for variable stars is an estimate (about magnitude 7 to 12.5), labelled as not yet measured.
  • Your own data stays unknown when it is. AstroLog never guesses a value a file doesn’t contain.

Privacy

Your pictures stay yours.

What AstroPlanner reads, what it keeps, and the few things it sends anywhere else.

Describes the app as it runs today, on your own computer. Updated 10 Nov 2026.

Never leaves your computer

  • The image data in your Seestar FITS and Canon CR2 files: only each file’s header is read, in your browser
  • Siril’s working folders: only its small text files are read
  • Location coordinates inside your files: used to match a saved place, never stored

What it keeps

  • Saved places and equipment
  • Session plans
  • Facts from file headers: target, times, exposure, device
  • A small, metadata-free preview of each final picture
  • Numbers measured from a stack, and a small preview

Sent to other services

  • Open-Meteo: your saved place’s coordinates, for the forecast; a city name you search for
  • STScI Digitized Sky Survey: a target’s position in the sky (not yours), once per target

Not in AstroPlanner

  • No ads, analytics or tracking scripts
  • No fonts or scripts loaded from other sites
  • Your browser location only if you press “Use my location” in Settings

Pictures and stacks

When you add a final picture, AstroPlanner makes a preview about 1,600 pixels wide and a thumbnail, removes the picture’s metadata, and keeps only those. When you add a Siril stack, it is measured in memory (star size, noise, background, colour) and only the numbers and a small preview are kept.

Deleting

Everything AstroPlanner keeps about you sits in one database file and one preview folder on your computer. Deleting them removes it all. (A third folder caches the survey pictures of targets; nothing in it is yours.)

For the login design
With accounts, this page gains a section on what is stored with your account, where it is hosted, and how to delete it. It is written once login is designed, not before.

Terms

Plain terms.

AstroPlanner is free, for personal use, and offered as it is.

Draft: plain-language summary, not legal advice. Review before a public launch.

Free, and staying that way

The forecasts come from Open-Meteo’s free service, which is for non-commercial use, and several data sources allow personal or non-profit use only. So AstroPlanner can’t be sold or used commercially.

Planning help, not a promise

Scores, windows and forecasts are estimates. Skies change, forecasts are wrong, and your own site may differ. Check conditions yourself, especially before travelling to a dark site.

Your files are your own

You keep every right to your pictures and data. AstroPlanner only stores the previews and facts described in Privacy, to show them back to you.

Other services’ terms

Data from Open-Meteo, OpenNGC, the Digitized Sky Survey and AAVSO VSX is used under their own terms, listed on Credits & data.

Changes

If these terms change, the date at the top changes too.
